Shorebirds are also known as waders. The black-tailed godwit is [critically endangered in Norway](https://lister.artsdatabanken.no/rodlisteforarter/2021/28731), with an estimated 34–64 breeding individuals in the 2021 Norwegian Red List assessment.

### Black-tailed godwit

The black-tailed godwit is a long-legged wader with a long, straight bill and a white wing bar in flight.

### Bar-tailed godwit

The bar-tailed godwit is a powerful migrant with a slightly upturned bill and barred tail.

### Eurasian curlew

The Eurasian curlew is Europe's largest wader and is recognised by its long, downcurved bill.

### Common greenshank

The common greenshank is an elegant wader with long greenish legs and a slightly upturned bill.

### Red knot

The red knot is a compact Arctic-breeding wader that gathers in large flocks outside the breeding season.

### Green sandpiper

The green sandpiper is a dark-backed wader often seen alone beside small freshwater pools and streams.

### Dunlin

The dunlin is a small, abundant wader whose breeding plumage includes a distinctive black belly patch.

### Common redshank

The common redshank is named for its bright red-orange legs and gives a ringing alarm call.

### Wood sandpiper

The wood sandpiper is a graceful migrant that favours shallow freshwater wetlands.

### Common sandpiper

The common sandpiper characteristically bobs its rear body and flies low over the water on stiff wings.

### Northern lapwing

The northern lapwing has a long crest, broad rounded wings and an iridescent green back.
